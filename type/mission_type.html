integer, public :: epoch_variable_mode = 1

the epoch variables (each node's epoch) in the x vector:

  • 1 - the epochs (days from the reference epoch), each scaled by magnitude(2*t0) of its initial value (so the scale grows along the trajectory, and the later epochs are cheaper to change)
  • 2 - the offsets from the initial guess (days), all scaled by the same epoch_scale (so a change of an epoch costs the same anywhere along the trajectory, and the variables start at 0)
real(kind=wp), public :: epoch_scale = 10.0_wp

scale for the epoch variables with epoch_variable_mode=2 [days] (10 days works well for both 100 and 1120 revs; much smaller values don't converge, and 100 days lets the 100-rev solutions drift)

logical, public, dimension(:), allocatable :: x_is_epoch

the opt vars that are epochs
integer, public :: xscale_mode = 1

the scales of the opt vars:

  • 1 - from the magnitudes of the initial values (see initialize_the_mission; the states are scaled by at least xscale_x0)
  • 2 - from the sensitivities: each scale is divided by the norm of its column of the (scaled) Jacobian at the initial guess, so each variable's unit change gives the same size change in the constraints (see equilibrate_scales)
real(kind=wp), public :: epoch_scale_factor = 1.0_wp

procedure, public :: equilibrate_scales

  • public subroutine equilibrate_scales(me, x)

    Set the opt var scales from the sensitivities (xscale_mode=2): the Jacobian is computed at x (as the solver does, see jacobian_mode), and each variable's scale is divided by the norm of its column, so the columns of the scaled Jacobian all have unit norm. A unit change of any scaled variable then gives the same size change in the (scaled) constraints, so the solver's minimum-norm steps weigh each variable by its effect, not its units. (With the scales from the magnitudes, the 1e5 km position scale makes the positions at periapsis, where they are most sensitive, the cheapest variables to change.) The epoch scales are also multiplied by epoch_scale_factor.
